# Theoretical Foundation

## Engineering Foundation

This monograph is grounded in the engineering principle that
professional analytical systems should be evaluated not only by the
quality of their outputs but also by the reliability of their execution
process.

Accordingly, execution is treated as an architectural process rather
than an implicit capability of a language model.

## Specification-Centered Execution

Within the Behmanesh Structural Index (BSI), the formal analytical
specification constitutes the primary source of authority throughout
execution.

Execution quality is therefore evaluated according to the degree to
which the implemented analytical process preserves the original
specification.

## Execution Governance

BSI introduces an execution-governance architecture that structures the
relationship between the user-defined specification and the language
model.

Rather than relying solely upon the internal reasoning behavior of the
model, execution is governed through explicit architectural mechanisms
designed to preserve framework fidelity, consistency, and analytical
quality.

## Framework Fidelity

Framework Fidelity represents an architectural property describing the
degree to which an executed analytical workflow conforms to the
original specification.

It is evaluated independently of the intrinsic capability of the
underlying language model.

## Architectural Evidence

Observed execution behaviors documented throughout this volume,
including Interpretation Drift, are interpreted as engineering evidence
supporting the need for execution-governance mechanisms.

They should not be interpreted as comprehensive explanations of the
internal operation of Large Language Models.
