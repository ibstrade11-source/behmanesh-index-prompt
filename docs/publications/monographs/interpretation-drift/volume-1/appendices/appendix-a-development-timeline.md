# Appendix A

# Development Timeline

## Purpose

This appendix documents the architectural evolution that ultimately resulted in the interpretation drift mitigation architecture adopted within the Behmanesh Structural Index (BSI).

The objective is to distinguish clearly between:

- architectural discovery,
- engineering validation,
- implementation,
- publication.

---

## Phase 1 — Framework Development

Development of BSI established framework-oriented analytical execution as a primary architectural objective.

Execution fidelity became an engineering concern from the earliest design stages.

---

## Phase 2 — Initial Architectural Observations

Repeated analytical interactions revealed recurring structural deviations during framework execution.

These observations motivated further investigation.

---

## Phase 3 — Architectural Hypothesis

Interpretation drift was formulated as an engineering hypothesis explaining the recurring execution patterns.

This hypothesis preceded comparative validation.

---

## Phase 4 — Cross-LLM Validation

Controlled comparative studies evaluated whether similar execution behaviors appeared across independent LLM implementations.

Results strengthened confidence in the architectural hypothesis.

---

## Phase 5 — Execution Engine Design

Validation results informed refinement of the BSI execution engine.

Framework Fidelity and Epistemic Integrity became explicit architectural objectives.

---

## Phase 6 — Engineering Documentation

The present monograph documents the complete engineering lifecycle from observation through implementation.
